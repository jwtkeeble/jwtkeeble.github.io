Classical simulation of quantum many-body systems is challenged by the combined effects of entanglement and non-stabilizerness (magic). Non-local magic, *M*<sup>NL</sup><sub>α</sub>, quantifies the component of non-stabilizerness that cannot be removed by local unitary transformations and provides a measure of quantum complexity beyond entanglement alone. It is bounded from below by the antiflatness of the entanglement spectrum, *F*<sub>A</sub>. We calculate stabilizer Rényi entropies, non-local magic, entanglement-spectrum antiflatness, and entanglement entropy in shell-model ground states of more than 260 even-even, odd-even, and odd-odd nuclei, using a bipartition into proton and neutron sectors.

We find that the previously established bound 4*F*<sub>A</sub> ≤ *M*<sup>NL</sup><sub>lin</sub> holds for every nucleus studied and approaches saturation in the limit of separable states. Odd-odd nuclei exhibit markedly different patterns of entanglement and magic compared with even-even and odd-even systems, revealing distinctive signatures of proton-neutron quantum correlations and providing new insight into the complexity generated by nuclear interactions.
