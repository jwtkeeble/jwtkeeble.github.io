I'm a computational physicist and research software engineer with a Ph.D. in theoretical physics from the University of Surrey. I build machine-learning models and high-performance scientific software. I'm currently an independent researcher working with Bielefeld University, after a postdoc there building multi-GPU neural-network simulations of atomic nuclei in JAX and GPU eigensolvers in Julia/CUDA.

I'm open to new roles in machine learning and scientific computing.

**[Download CV (PDF)](/files/cv.pdf)**

## Highlights

- **GPU-scale scientific code:** custom CUDA.jl kernels and a re-engineered MCMC sampler that took our quantum-resource calculations from 24 to 44 qubits (10⁵ to 10⁹ sampled states), cutting runtimes from months to hours.
- **Better optimisers:** Decisional Gradient Descent, a game-theoretic extension of stochastic reconfiguration that outperforms it ([paper](/publication/2024-01-31-second-order-optimisation), [software](/software/)).
- **PyTorch Pace-Setter Award, 2023:** [first recipient](https://pytorch.org/ecosystem/contributor-awards-2023/#:~:text=PYTORCH%20PACE%2DSETTER,James%20Keeble) of the PyTorch Foundation's contributor award.

**Tools:** Python · PyTorch · JAX · Julia · CUDA.jl · HPC (SLURM, Apptainer) · CI/CD

## Research

I use neural networks to represent quantum many-body wavefunctions of atomic nuclei. I study how entanglement and non-stabilizerness ("magic") limit classical simulation, and I develop optimisers that make variational Monte Carlo training faster and more stable.

See [Publications](/publications/), [Talks](/talks/) and [Software](/software/) for details.
